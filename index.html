Can agents build the mapper that state machine learning needs?

Black-box state machine learning finds protocol bugs, but every study pays weeks of expert work per mapper: the adapter that turns abstract inputs into real, encrypted packets. That is why studies cover 3–19 implementations. We ran both arms of the experiment on SSH in one day.

Yes, and it can be trusted if every claim is replayed. An agent-built mapper, written by Codex against tests written first, matched the published hand-built mapper on every server behaviour the two share, carried fewer harness errors into its models, and scaled to 12 implementations. It found a current RFC 4253 MUST NOT violation in wolfSSH and three more candidate behaviours. Both arms also produced plausible but wrong models; only live replay caught them.

Two arms, one question

Independent authors, code bases, agents, key exchange and authentication; merged into one repository.

Arm 1

Agent-built mapper

Alan Yuldashev (@Lumberj3ck) · repository root
  • Claude wrote the spec and validation tests first; Codex wrote the SSH mapper from the RFCs, no SSH library (~330 lines).
  • One Codex agent per implementation onboarded 8 targets in two parallel waves; all 12 targets pass one 16-test suite.
  • RFC catalogue: 15 bug patterns as automata, each with its quoted MUST/SHOULD, written by a Claude subagent.
  • AALpy KV learner, protocol-seeded parallel equivalence oracle, 32-vCPU cloud VM.
  • Every catalogue hit replayed 3× on a fresh container; refuted hits fed back to the learner.
  • curve25519, password auth, strict KEX on.
Arm 2

Published mapper

Ansorjon Juraev (@TheWingTheWong) · reference-harness/
  • Reuses Fiterau-Brostean et al.'s ssh-fuzzer (SPIN 2017 / NDSS 2023) with 5 documented patches.
  • Validated first: the original authors' published traces replay symbol for symbol.
  • OpenSSH 10.0p1, Dropbear 2025.88, Go x/crypto 0.58.0; 39 divergence points in 9 classes, each replayed and attacked by skeptic-review subagents and an independent paramiko client.
  • Seven more servers and an 18-version replay sweep (observations, not yet skeptic-reviewed).
  • Measured the cost of the loop: where an agent sufficed, where a human was needed.
  • DH group14, public-key auth, strict KEX off.

Cross-validation of the two harnesses

Both harnesses on the same OpenSSH 10.0p1, Dropbear 2025.88 and Go x/crypto 0.58.0 containers (reference-harness/results/v2/A/summary.md).

32 learned-model disagreement classes, each replayed live with both mappers and attributed:

published mapper 26 setup 4 2
Published mapper: a close reported one step late, stream corruption after a stray NEWKEYS, a one-channel cap (documented limits). Setup: automatic KEXINIT. Agent-built: one Go model merged states across a pre-auth rekey — caught by our own replay step and removed by relearning with the refuted witness as a seed.
  • 0 server-level disagreements: the harnesses never disagreed about what a server did with the same bytes; 120 of 120 KEXINIT-first replay pairs agree at every step.
  • Every published-arm class D1–D9 is reproduced by the agent-built harness; with strict KEX, D7 and the initial-KEX part of D9 disappear, as predicted.
  • The agent-built RFC catalogue picks out the same implementations as the hand-written D1–D9 classes; 62 of 62 witnesses confirmed live.

New findings (candidates)

Found by the agent-built arm, confirmed by deterministic replay, root cause traced in source by a Codex agent. Severity is low or not assessed; none is an on-path attack.

CANDIDATE-003 · wolfSSH 1.6.0

During a key re-exchange — after its own KEXINIT, before its NEWKEYS — wolfSSH answers authentication requests (USERAUTH_FAILURE and even USERAUTH_SUCCESS) and opens channels. OpenSSH and MINA hold the reply until after NEWKEYS; Go and russh disconnect.

Still present on wolfSSH master d043c69. Cause: the dispatcher's keying-state gate only covers messages 1–49.

Found by the RFC catalogue on the learned model → replayed → source-traced. findings/CANDIDATE-003-*.md
spec ambiguitysince 9.6 (2023)

CANDIDATE-001 · OpenSSH 9.2p1 and 10.6

Under strict KEX, a NEWKEYS received outside any key exchange resets the receive sequence number while the old keys stay; sshd answers UNIMPLEMENTED and continues. Nine other implementations disconnect.

Root cause: packet.c resets the counter before dispatch decides the message is unexpected. Needs a misbehaving peer; informational/low.

Codex refused to force a failing test green and reported it; confirmed by a three-way experiment and upstream source.
informational

CANDIDATE-002 · libssh 0.12.2

A stray NEWKEYS puts the session into a terminal error state without DISCONNECT; later packets are dropped before decryption. In our server the socket stayed open ≥ 120 s; other connections were unaffected.

CANDIDATE-004 · Apache MINA SSHD 2.20.1

After authentication, a SERVICE_REQUEST for ssh-connection is accepted and replaces the connection service: the open channel is orphaned and its CHANNEL_CLOSE is never answered. A new channel works.

Published-mapper arm: how OpenSSH, Dropbear and Go disagree

On the normal handshake the three agree exactly. Off the normal path: 39 divergence points in 9 classes, each replayed 5–10× (0 nondeterministic), skeptic-reviewed and reproduced with an independent client.

A NEWKEYS sent outside any key exchange, after a completed handshake, across all 12 implementations of the agent-built arm (results/seqnr/).

The published mapper filters exactly this situation out as "stream corruption"; the agent-built mapper handles it, which is how the OpenSSH behaviour surfaced.

What went wrong, and what it taught us

Plausible models can be wrong

Our catalogue reported an authentication bypass in Go x/crypto/ssh. Replay refuted it: the learner never queried that path and merged two states. The published arm hit the same failure twice. Lesson: trust replayed sequences, never raw models.

Agents that stop are worth more

Three times a Codex agent refused to make a failing test pass and reported a real behaviour instead: AsyncSSH's interleaved IGNOREs, OpenSSH's counter reset, and Go/libssh omitting the strict-KEX marker on rekey.

The loop found bugs in itself

Our mapper re-used keys after a refused rekey, dropped strict KEX on rekey, and missed an algorithm name. Two catalogue patterns were too strict. All were caught by replays and fixed against tests.

Open at submission

Dropbear and AsyncSSH accept unlimited channels, so their models never converged; the one-channel bound is specified but not implemented. Strict-KEX handshake conformance of 10 servers is already published (CCS 2025) — our novelty is scale, the auth/connection/rekey layers and new implementations.

Demo script (about 5 minutes)

  1. 0:00 The bottleneck. Mappers cost weeks per implementation, so studies stop at 3–19. Can an agent build one — and can we trust it?
  2. 0:45 Two arms. Same question, two teammates: Codex builds the mapper from the RFCs vs the published expert mapper, validated against its authors' own traces.
  3. 1:30 The headline. 0 server-level disagreements; model errors 2 (agent) vs 26 (published); 62/62 witnesses confirmed. Show the bar.
  4. 2:15 Calibration. Known bugs found blind: Erlang CVE-2025-32433 (vulnerable vs patched), CCS 2025 Dropbear, a wolfSSH bug fixed upstream 26 days ago.
  5. 3:00 The new finding. wolfSSH answers auth and opens channels in the middle of a rekey — RFC 4253 §7.1 MUST NOT, still on master. Live: scripts/replay.py wolfssh "KEXINIT KEX30 NEWKEYS SR_AUTH UA_PW_OK KEXINIT CH_OPEN".
  6. 3:45 Honesty. The fake Go auth bypass, refuted by replay. Agents that stopped instead of cheating. What did not converge.
  7. 4:30 Next. One-channel bound, post-quantum KEX, a second protocol (MQTT 5 / CoAP), and reports to maintainers.
